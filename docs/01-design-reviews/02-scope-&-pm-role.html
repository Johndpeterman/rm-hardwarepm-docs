02 Scope & PM Role

General overview of the two major design reviews:

PDR CDR
Decision Is the architecture ready to continue designing? Is the design ready for manufacturing?
CAD - top-level design
- major systems
- interfaces		 CAD is in state to begin manufacturing: parts finished, clearances, serviceability, etc.
Evidence Prelim. CAD, known requirements and considerations, understand interfaces Final CAD version, BOM, drawings, assembly and manufacturing plans
Results Robot architecture is in place, and can start designing towards manufacturing Begin manufacturing

What belongs in our PDR:

  • Remember this is about correctness and feasibility, not at the detail of manufacturing
    • This is point that has harmed us in the past-we go through the design process without stopping point to determine if it should reach a manufacturing stage

PDR should show, for example:

  • What robot must do/meeting our defined requirements
  • Ultimately adhering to known comp requirements
  • Concepts and exactly why one was selected
  • Top level CAD --> refer here for more details
  • Major subsystems and their interactions
    • Mechanical & electrical interfaces!!
  • Size, mass, CoG, loading, deflection--parameters like these should be considered here
  • Prototyping/test benches
  • Plans to reach CDR (CDR = manufacturable design)

Leave this phase with the understanding that this given architecture will likely work, and any unresolved details have a path to completion.

What belongs in our CDR:

  • This is about having a design ready for building

Should show, for example:

  • Finalized onshape version/branch
  • Complete assemblies/parts
  • Materials/components to be purchased (BOM ready)
  • Part drawings for manufacturing
  • Assembly order
  • Serviceability (I'm begging you)
  • Fabrication plans

Leave this phase with means to bring the design to manufacturing, and evidence that it will assemble and be integrated as expected.

Doesn't necessarily mean every uncertainty is gone but they are at least recognized.

My Role:

I'll provide my specific advice from my experience as a designer, but I'm not going to pretend I know more about a subsystem than whoever's working on it. I'm responsible for asking questions that make it clear to us how this project is developing, and if it's developing in the correct direction.

  • What requirement does this thing work towards?
    • What assumptions exist
  • What is the evidence that supports a design decision
  • Interfaces and what downstream work does the thing affect
  • What must be solved/tested before we trust a design to work
